A Secondary Market for Unused AI Usage

A three-stage proposal for Grok subscriptions: credit already-paid usage when a subscriber upgrades, allow unused usage blocks to be resold inside the platform under transparent, rule-based pricing, and later offer futures on those blocks.

Executive summary

  1. Problem. Subscription usage that is paid for but not consumed expires without value, and upgrading mid-cycle does not account for what was already paid.
  2. Stage 1. Credit the unused, already-paid portion of the current plan toward an upgrade. Low complexity, immediate fairness gain.
  3. Stage 2. Pilot an in-platform market for standard usage blocks under an 80/20 rule: 80% of the blocks offered are sold at a fixed xAI price that declines toward expiry but never below the plan floor, and 20% trade freely at a market price with a floor and no ceiling. xAI charges a fee per block sold (example value: 4 points of list price).
  4. Stage 3. Once the market has stable data, offer futures: blocks bought in advance, at a discount, for activation on a future date.
  5. Outcome. Lower effective cost for light users, cheaper burst capacity for heavy users, and new transaction revenue and retention for the platform, without external resale.
1

The problem

AI subscriptions are sold as fixed periods of capacity, while real usage is irregular. The gap between what is paid for and what is used is currently lost.

Waste

Unused capacity expires

Usage that is not consumed within the period disappears. The subscriber pays for it and nobody benefits from it.

Upgrades

Paid usage is not credited

Moving to a higher tier mid-cycle does not reflect the unused, already-paid portion of the current plan.

Mismatch

Demand is uneven

Some subscribers hit their limits while others leave capacity idle. There is no mechanism to reallocate it.

2

The proposal in three stages

The proposal is deliberately incremental. Stage 1 is a policy change; Stage 2 introduces a market only after Stage 1 is in place; Stage 3 adds futures once the market has stable data.

Stage 1

Upgrade credit

When a subscriber upgrades, the unused, already-paid portion of the current plan is credited toward the new plan. No new infrastructure beyond billing logic.

Policy change
Stage 2

Usage block market

Subscribers can list unused usage as standard blocks. Most blocks are sold at a fixed xAI price; a share trades freely at market price. The platform sets the rules, the floor and the fee per block sold.

Controlled pilot
Stage 3

Futures

Subscribers can buy blocks in advance, at a discount, for activation on a future date. Introduced only after the block market has stable data (section 6).

Later extension
3

Standard usage blocks

To make usage tradable, it is packaged into standard, fungible blocks. Each block has a fixed size, a tier and an expiry date.

  • Blocks are defined by the platform, not by users, so every block of the same tier is interchangeable.
  • Blocks stay within their tier: Plus blocks trade in the Plus market, Ultra blocks in the Ultra market.
  • Each block carries its own expiry, which drives its price as it approaches.

4

Pricing mechanism

Prices follow a transparent 80/20 rule. 80% of the blocks offered are sold at a fixed xAI price that declines as expiry approaches, never below the plan floor. The remaining 20% trade freely at a market price driven by demand, protected by a floor and with no ceiling.

4.1

80/20 rule

Of the blocks offered, 80% are sold at the fixed xAI price and 20% trade freely at market price. This keeps prices predictable while letting demand signal real value. xAI charges a fee per block sold, for example 4 points of list price.

4.2

Floor, no ceiling

A minimum price per tier prevents dumping. There is no upper limit, so scarce capacity can reach its real value.

4.3

Time decay

The xAI price declines as expiry approaches, but never below the plan floor, which encourages early listing and steady supply.

4.4 Simultaneous versus staggered expiry

If every block expires on the same day, supply arrives all at once and prices collapse to the floor. Staggering expiry across the period smooths supply.

Simultaneous expiry

All blocks reach expiry on day 7: a single supply spike and prices at the floor.

Staggered expiry

Blocks expire on different days: supply is spread evenly and prices stay stable.

5

Market flow

All transactions happen inside the platform. No usage leaves the ecosystem and no external payment rails are involved.

1. ListThe seller lists one or more unused blocks.
2. PriceThe rule sets the price from time to expiry and demand.
3. MatchA buyer on the same tier acquires the block.
4. SettleUsage moves to the buyer; credit goes to the seller.
6

Futures (Stage 3)

Once the spot market is stable, subscribers could buy blocks that activate in a future week at a discount, giving the platform predictable demand.

    8

    Anti-abuse safeguards

    A market for usage only works if it cannot be used for arbitrage, laundering or automated farming. The following rules would apply from the pilot onward.

    8.1

    Only paid subscribers can list or buy blocks.

    8.2

    Blocks trade only within the same tier.

    8.3

    Sellers receive platform credit, not cash.

    8.4

    Credit cannot be withdrawn or transferred outside the platform.

    8.5

    Accounts must be in good standing for a minimum period before listing.

    8.6

    Listing limits per account and per period.

    8.7

    Purchase limits per account and per period.

    8.8

    Purchased blocks cannot be resold.

    8.9

    The xAI price is set by rule, and the market price cannot fall below the floor of its tier.

    8.10

    Identity and payment-method checks to detect multi-accounting.

    8.11

    Automated monitoring for unusual trading patterns.

    8.12

    The platform can suspend the market or adjust parameters at any time.

    10

    Open risks

    RiskDescription and mitigation
    CannibalizationSome upgrades could be replaced by block purchases. Tier boundaries and limits mitigate this.
    Compute planningResold usage is consumed rather than expiring, which increases realized load.
    Regulatory treatmentCredits and resale may have accounting, tax or consumer-law implications by jurisdiction.
    ComplexityA market adds product surface. A limited pilot keeps it contained.
    11

    Implementation roadmap

    Each stage is independent and can be evaluated before moving to the next.

    Stage 1

    Basic fix: upgrade credit

    Credit the unused, already-paid portion of the plan on upgrade. Billing change only.

    Stage 2

    Block market pilot

    Standard blocks, an 80/20 rule (80% at the xAI price, 20% at market price with a floor), a fee per block sold (example value: 4 points of list price) and safeguards, for a limited group of subscribers.

    Stage 3

    Futures

    Discounted blocks for future activation, once the spot market has stable data.

    12

    Open questions for xAI

    1. Is crediting unused, paid usage on upgrade technically feasible in the current billing system?
    2. What block size and expiry window best match actual usage patterns?
    3. What floor and fee levels would protect both sellers and platform revenue?
    4. Should credit be usable only for usage, or also for subscription renewals?
    5. Which jurisdictions would require specific review before a pilot?
    6. What metrics would define a successful pilot?
